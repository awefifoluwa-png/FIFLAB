# Source references and boundaries

The app links to publisher/repository pages for these specific editions. Their titles and broad scope were verified on 17 November 2026. The list does not claim that these are the latest editions. No standard text is redistributed, no detailed prescribed specimen preparation is inferred, and no software calculation is certified as compliance.

1. ASTM International. **ASTM C109/C109M-24.** Standard Test Method for Compressive Strength of Hydraulic Cement Mortars (Using 50 mm [2 in.] Cube Specimens). https://store.astm.org/c0109_c0109m-24.html
2. ASTM International. **ASTM C1012/C1012M-24a.** Standard Test Method for Length Change of Hydraulic-Cement Mortars Exposed to a Sulfate Solution. https://store.astm.org/c1012_c1012m-24a.html
3. ASTM International. **ASTM C876-22b.** Standard Test Method for Corrosion Potentials of Uncoated Reinforcing Steel in Concrete. https://store.astm.org/c0876-22b.html
4. Yeh, I. (1998). **Concrete Compressive Strength [Dataset].** UCI Machine Learning Repository. DOI: 10.24432/C5PK67. https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/165/concrete-compressive-strength . UCI lists CC BY 4.0. This published dataset is externally linked, not included in the illustrative project and not automatically transformed into the app's data schema.

The mathematical engine uses explicit mass-ratio, additive-volume, geometry and descriptive-statistics equations. Independently checked numerical examples are documented in CALCULATIONS.md and encoded in tests/. These equations alone do not imply conformity with a standard.

Synthetic measurements in the example project are generated deterministically by src/domain/defaults.ts. They are original demonstration records, not extracted from any of these references. There are no fabricated experimental results or research publications attributed to Fifoluwa Awe.
